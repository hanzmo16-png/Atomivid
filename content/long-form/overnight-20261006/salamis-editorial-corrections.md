## Corrections resolved by research

| Wording risk identified in prior review | Editorial correction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Implying Persian naval power was destroyed in one battle | Distinguish defeat and withdrawal from elimination of the fleet | Herodotus 8.130 describes surviving ships wintering at Cyme and gathering at Samos the following spring |
| Implying Athens recovered securely within months | Remove the recovery deadline and acknowledge renewed occupation | Herodotus 9.3 describes Mardonius taking the evacuated city again; 9.13 describes further destruction |
| Treating Salamis as the end of the invasion | Retain its turning-point significance while naming the subsequent campaign | Tuplin's Salamis article places Plataea and Mycale alongside the naval victory as necessary outcomes |

## Proposed payoff narration

Salamis was a turning point, not the end of the war. Xerxes withdrew, but Persian forces remained a threat. Herodotus describes surviving ships gathering again the next spring. Athens would be occupied once more, and the struggle continued into the following year. Plataea and Mycale still lay ahead. What the allies had won in these waters was not a world without danger. They had kept the resistance alive. A great fleet had entered a confined battlefield, and the Greeks had denied it the victory it expected. The sea had changed the campaign. It had not finished it.

This paragraph replaces the overclaim; it is not an extra epilogue automatically appended to paid narration. Measure its duration after editing the actual beat. The final two sentences are editorial synthesis, not a quotation or a claim about every Persian sailor's expectations.

## Sources checked

Godley translation hosted by University of Chicago: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Herodotus/8C*.html — ancient account of withdrawal, remaining army and fleet. Attribute it; ancient testimony is not an exact modern operational record.
- Herodotus, Histories 9.3 and 9.13: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Herodotus/9A*.html — subsequent occupation and destruction of Athens. No claim of uninterrupted rapid recovery.
- Christopher J. Tuplin, “Salamis,” Encyclopaedia Iranica: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/salamis/ — scholarly synthesis of significance, later victories and disputed tactical reconstruction.
